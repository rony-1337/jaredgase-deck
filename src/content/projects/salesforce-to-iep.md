## The situation

Intuit's TurboTax experts were stuck on aging Salesforce workflows that slowed
everything down. Tools were unreliable and inconsistent. Customer data was
often stale or missing. The flows did not match how experts actually worked,
and during peak tax season, the busiest moment of the year, the failures landed
straight on customers.

This was not a small group. Thirty thousand plus experts, spanning several very
different roles: partner and outsourced agents, Intuit-badged staff, and Tier 2
leads and subject matter experts. All of them resolving complex technical and
account issues at high volume, all of them needing tools they could trust to
give fast, accurate help.

## What I was asked to do

Lead design end to end to unify expert tooling inside IEP, Intuit's own expert
platform, and replace Salesforce with something durable and scalable. The bar
was specific. Improve the workflows and cut friction. Align across every role
and partner team. Ship the capabilities before peak tax season. And do all of
it with no drop in expert performance or customer outcomes during the switch.

## How I worked the problem

**Research first, in the field.** I ran follow-me-homes with real experts,
service blueprinting to map the dependencies between teams and tools, and end to
end journey mapping of how a call actually flows. That surfaced the real
breakpoints in the Salesforce workflows and let me prioritize where IEP could
help most.

**A framework to scope the whole system.** A migration this size is unworkable
if you treat every screen as net new. So I built a capability framework that
sorted every workflow into one of three buckets: ready to reuse as is, needs
strengthening for durability, or genuinely missing and needs to be built. That
single decision rule kept hundreds of pieces of scope honest.

**One map everyone could align on.** From there I built a unified capability map
of every workflow an expert touches during a call, with ownership, maturity,
risk, and dependencies on each one. It doubled as a roadmap and a risk register,
and it became the artifact that kept product, engineering, and leadership
pointed the same way.

**Design execution across dozens of capabilities.** With the system mapped, I
designed or strengthened the actual workflows: engagement creation, escalation,
global search, diagnostic lookup, multi-tab, the commerce features experts use
for orders, refunds, billing, and vouchers, plus the launchpad and onboarding
flows new experts start with.

**Past parity, with AI.** A migration alone is not innovation, so I did not stop
at matching Salesforce. On the old system, moving a customer from Tier 1 to
Tier 2 meant a manual chat handoff with no context carried over, the expert
re-entering things the system already knew, and up to two minutes of delay every
time. The new design added gen-AI self-help and de-escalation, an automatic call
summary and context that travels with the customer, and attribute-based routing
straight to the right Tier 2 expert. Faster connections, fewer escalations, and
real AI partnership instead of a like-for-like rebuild.

**Validated with the people who use it.** I ran multiple expert roundtables,
synthesized feedback across roles and partners, and iterated on the patterns
that kept coming up. I used AI-powered tooling to speed the synthesis so I could
spend the time on decisions, not transcription.

**Shipped it without breaking peak season.** I partnered tightly with
engineering through build and launch: detailed specs and edge cases, daily build
reviews for accuracy, pre-production testing where we actively tried to break
things, and UAT scenarios run with real experts, all to make sure it held up
under high-load peak season.

## The results

A high-impact launch with numbers behind it. Average handle time dropped five
percent to 17.5 minutes, which at this scale is millions in savings. Transaction
NPS rose 1.1 points. Tools readiness reached 82 percent, eight points better
than Salesforce, the system it replaced. And the beyond-parity AI work cut
escalations by roughly 40 percent year over year while gen-AI self-help
deflected about a quarter of issues before a human was needed.

## What I took from it

This is the project I point to when someone asks how I lead complex, multi-team
platform work. It was grounded in field research, structured through frameworks
that are still used for cross-company work today, designed for durability with a
platform mindset, executed with tight cross-functional alignment, and measured
with outcomes that mattered. It also helped validate IEP as Intuit's long-term
expert platform and set scalable patterns for the roles that came after.
