## The situation

The Intuit Expert Portal had grown into a static, manual system. To handle a
single customer call, an expert moved across seven separate tools. Support
specialists and domain experts, the CPAs and bookkeepers, hit the same friction
all day. There was no unified view of the customer. Guidance sat off to the
side, divorced from the workflow it was supposed to help with. And the
swivel-chair was literal. Experts cleared cache and cookies about 2.7 times a
day just to keep the tools working.

The problem in the experts' words was simple. The platform is static and manual,
and the tools I need are not all in one place.

## What I was asked to do

Evolve IEP from its third-peak iteration into a next generation system built on
one idea. The work should come to the expert instead of the expert going to find
it. That meant rethinking the workspace itself, not just adding features to the
one that existed.

## How I worked the problem

**Fifteen rounds of research, not one.** Over five months I ran follow-me-homes,
in-depth interviews, Wizard of Oz role plays, and concept testing, with both
expert archetypes and with leadership. Repeated contact with real experts is
what kept a vision project honest.

**Four principles to steer the calls.** Deliver outcomes, not outputs.
Information at hand, never in the way. Be the hammer, not the house. And design
for imperfection, because the AI would not always be right and the design had to
hold up when it was wrong.

**Reset the baseline first.** Two structural moves cleared the ground. Intuit
Assist evolved into a persistent command center that consolidated scattered tabs
into a single engagement hub. Utilities moved into pop-out windows, which gave
experts control of their canvas and the option of a second monitor.

**The provocation: dynamic workflow cards.** The core move collapsed tools and
actions into real-time cards triggered by what the customer says, not by where
the expert clicks. Two capabilities made it work. The workflow built itself as
the call progressed, guiding the expert through contextual steps. And on-contact
guidance surfaced from the live conversation to match the immediate moment. The
cards followed clear rules. Conversation-driven from real statements,
action-oriented with a clear next step, sequential so only one showed at a time,
and adaptive when the conversation changed direction.

**Designed for when the AI is wrong.** I designed three scenarios so the
experience worked even with weak AI outputs. A clear utterance with accurate
guidance, the easy path. A sparse utterance with weak guidance. And a clear
utterance where the AI still misses. In the weak-guidance walkthrough, the system
surfaced an answer-the-question card flagged red to signal it did not have
enough to go on. Rather than fabricate a step, it kept the expert in control.
Once the customer clarified (they were looking for their 2025 estimated tax
vouchers), a proper guidance card replaced it, and every following action, the
screen share, the closure, the call-note approval, stayed inside the workflow as
cards, so there was no tool to switch to.

**What the research taught me.** Experts work with AI as a co-pilot. They
welcome a suggestion they trust and take the wheel the moment they doubt it.
Guidance has a Goldilocks problem. Too much or off-target and it gets ignored.
One expert called persistent low-value guidance the mother-in-law you want to
get rid of. And one size fits all does not work, which is why canvas control
mattered as much as the cards.

## The results

The specific designs did not ship unchanged. They shaped the iterations that
followed through the rest of 2025. The broader AI Native IEP program piloted
with 400 to 450 experts and then became the new baseline, retiring the legacy
UI.

Adoption was measured index to control, where 100 is parity with the old system.
Full Service tax domain experts came in at 361, TurboTax Live Assisted domain
experts at 140, and product support experts at 109. These are program-level
outcomes from a cross-functional effort, so I do not isolate them as individual
design attribution. The honest read is that the direction landed and the new
workspace outperformed the one it replaced.

## What I took from it

This is the project I point to for setting design direction on an ambiguous,
AI-native problem and moving a roadmap with it. It was grounded in fifteen rounds
of real research, structured by principles that survived contact with hard
scenarios, and deliberately designed for the moments the AI gets it wrong. The
throughline is the part I care about most. The system should bring the right
action to the expert at the right moment, but the expert always controls the
canvas and makes the final call.
