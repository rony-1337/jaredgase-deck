## The problem with most principles

Design principles usually die on a poster. Everyone nods, nobody uses them, and
the same debates repeat. I wanted a shared language for our Salesforce work that
people would actually reach for when making decisions.

## What I built

Five principles, ordered by priority: platform first, build with what exists,
make it feel native, inspire trust, and design for tomorrow. Each one comes with
practical guidance, the tradeoffs it implies, and real examples, paired with a
pattern library so a designer can go from principle to execution without
guessing. I reinforced them through working sessions so they landed as something
teams do, not something they read.

## Then I made them enforce themselves

The hard part of any framework is adoption without adding process. So I built
custom validators across ChatGPT, a Gemini Gem, and a Claude Project. A designer
or PM can run a PRD or a doc against the principles and get specific, actionable
feedback before design even begins: where it aligns, where the risk is, what is
missing. The principles stopped being a reference and became a tool that runs.

## Why it matters

This is the clearest example of two things I care about: scaling design across a
whole org, and using AI as real leverage rather than a talking point. It gave the
team the language and the confidence to scope work well and to push back, kindly,
on decisions that did not fit.
